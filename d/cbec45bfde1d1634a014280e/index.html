Start with the house you have

Older home.
Different possibilities.

A house by Lake Couchiching isn’t the same as a downtown home with rooms the ducts never reached.

EHI’s Orillia service page describes older homes and former summer places around Lake Couchiching and Lake Simcoe, including lakeside houses with baseboards and window air conditioners. It identifies ductless heat pumps as an option for winter heating and summer cooling.

For homes that already have some ductwork, the same page describes keeping ducts that work and adding a ductless head in rooms they do not reach. Your existing setup is the starting point—not a reason to assume every room needs the same solution.

Good questions to start with

Before you make
the change.

Your home’s suitability and equipment choice need a conversation with EHI. These answers explain the offer described on its official pages.

Can I consider a heat pump without existing ducts?

Yes. EHI lists Midea Hyper Heat ductless options specifically for homes without ducts, in single-zone and multi-zone configurations. Ask which arrangement suits your house.

Can the same ductless system provide summer cooling?

Yes. EHI’s Orillia page describes a ductless heat pump providing winter heating and summer cooling for lakeside homes that currently use baseboards and window units.

Is a new-installation assessment and quote free?

EHI states that assessments and quotes for new installations are free with no obligation. Repair diagnostic visits are different and have a service fee disclosed before booking.

What should I have ready when requesting a quote?

Have your heating fuel, approximate square footage, number of storeys and current equipment details handy. EHI also suggests a photo of the equipment label and any symptoms you have noticed.
